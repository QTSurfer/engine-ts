A backtest can scale in. A strategy in `ExecutionMode.LONG_MULTI` (`setMultiEntryEnabled(true)`), which the backtest used to store and ignore, now builds its position in steps, as the Java engine's multi-entry pipeline does. Every buy while long adds an entry to the one position, where before it was dropped as `already-long`. The first entry spends `buyPercent` of the cash; each further one spends `buyPercent` of the funds of the run (the initial funding plus what the positions closed so far made, as the Java engine sizes a backtest's entries) and never more than the entries of the open position have not locked (what a partial sell brings back is not funds again until the position closes). A sell sells `sellPercent` percent of what the position holds (100 by default; 0 is dropped as `zero-size`), and a partial sell leaves the rest open with its stops. The protective stop of an entry that asks for one is re-placed for the whole position, and one that asks for none leaves the existing stop covering all of it. The yield is one, when the position closes: what every sell brought in less what every entry cost; `session.trades` shows that as one trade at the average entry and exit price. A strategy in `LONG` runs exactly as before: one entry, and a sell closes the position whatever its `sellPercent`.
