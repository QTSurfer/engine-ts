A buy or sell signal can ask for its own size. `setSizePercent(percent)` takes a number from 0 to 100 (it throws for anything else, NaN included) and `getSizePercent()` reads it, `undefined` when the signal asks for none. It is the share of its own base the signal trades: on a buy, the percent of the funds the entry spends, never more than what the open entries have not locked, which replaces the run's `buyPercent` for that signal only (a size of 0 buys nothing and is dropped as `zero-size`); on a sell, the percent of the open position, as `sellPercent` was. It works in `LONG` and `LONG_MULTI`, so a strategy can buy 10% of the funds, then 20%, then everything that is left. `setSellPercent` stays as the sell side's name and writes the same number, without validating it; `getSellPercent()` is the size, or 100 when there is none. `MarketHintSignal.KEY_SELL_PERCENT` (`"_sellPct"`) is replaced by `KEY_SIZE_PERCENT` (`"_sizePct"`). This mirrors the Java engine.

Next to it, `setMaxPercent(percent)` / `getMaxPercent()` ask for a share, from 0 to 100, of the most the signal can trade. On a buy that is a share of what the run allows an entry: the `buyPercent` share of the funds, or all that is left unlocked when `buyPercent` is 100, and never more than what is left. 100 is that maximum, `buyPercent` is a ceiling it never passes (a `sizePercent` takes its place), and 0 buys nothing. On a sell the most is the open position, so it means what `sizePercent` means. A signal has one size: `setSizePercent`, `setMaxPercent` and `setSellPercent` each take back the others, and `getSellPercent()` reads `sizePercent`, else `maxPercent`, else 100.
